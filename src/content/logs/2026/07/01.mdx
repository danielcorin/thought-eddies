Ten years ago, I used to sit at a long table with my software engineering team in our office. It's hard to quantify the value I got from sitting at that table. It wasn't only because we were in-person. That helped. It was the mentorship I received just from rolling my chair over to my tech lead or a teammate and asking questions day after day. It was hard not to learn new things in that environment.

At remote companies, mentorship is no longer the default. It needs to be scheduled.

And sometimes, even if you're in-person, the mentor you need isn't in the room.

This is especially challenging now as AI changes the software industry. If you feel like you're drowning in content about AI, you're not alone. The hardest part right now about learning to use AI is finding the right content at the right level. Easier said than done if you don't yet know what you don't know. Even the best article or YouTube video will only teach you a few new things. Can you justify spending those 22 minutes on 2x speed?

In my last role, I leaned heavily into teaching AI tools in addition to my work as a software engineer. Whether they were a seasoned engineer or had never written a line of code, I met with anyone who was curious and interested in learning. I'm excited to be opening this up publicly through wvlen. If you're looking for social proof, some really nice folks have written reviews of their experience on https://wvlen.llc.
